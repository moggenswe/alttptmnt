# Let the menu gate, let the ammo flow freely

**Keep Missile Pack and Rope Pack as progression, but let the area select menu be the gate itself and let the game's own pickups be bonus ammo.** Do not spend time zeroing the counters continuously, do not remove vanilla pickups and do not build progressive capacity. The reason is that missiles only exist in Area 3 and that our Lua menu already refuses to load Area 3 without both packs. So the packs are not "fake progression" as long as the menu is the only way in. The real problem lies elsewhere. Area 4 has a rope section but no rope requirement in the logic. The client only refills on an area load, so anyone who fires off all their missiles or takes a continue can be left standing at 0. And viewers currently have no way of seeing what is missing for go mode. Before the stream we should first and foremost do four things. Settle the Area 4 question. Introduce a floor refill ("has the pack and the counter is 0 → refill"). Show received items in BizHawk. Build a shared go mode panel for OBS that shows both Ganon and Shredder. The core package is roughly **2.5–3 days of work**. A dedicated PopTracker pack for TMNT is nice but can wait.

## The game hands out 30 missiles and 9 ropes for free, but only in two places

The starting point is how little ammunition the vanilla game contains and where it is. The pickup table in our ROM is read with the layout from abyssonym's tmnt_rando. It shows that **missiles only exist in Area 3: three pickups of +10 each**. Ropes exist in **Area 3 (two pickups of +3 each) and Area 4 (a single pickup of +3)**. Areas 1, 2, 5 and 6 have neither missiles nor ropes ([tmnt_rando item_table.txt](https://github.com/abyssonym/tmnt_rando/blob/master/tables/item_table.txt), [item_pointers.txt](https://github.com/abyssonym/tmnt_rando/blob/master/tables/item_pointers.txt); verified against the project's ROM). Both counters are clamped to 99 by a shared addition routine. Every missile shot and every rope throw costs exactly 1. Enemies cannot drop either, since the drops are boomerang, shuriken or triple shuriken ([TASVideos Game Resources](https://tasvideos.org/GameResources/NES/TeenageMutantNinjaTurtles)). There is also no way to lose because of an ammo shortage. The firing code simply does nothing when the counter is 0.

Two properties drive the design. The first is that **pickups respawn when you leave a building and go back in**. So a player inside Area 3 can always fetch more, and StrategyWiki explicitly advises a second trip to the missile building because ten "may not be enough" ([StrategyWiki Area 3](https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Teenage_Mutant_Ninja_Turtles/Area_3)). The second is that **Area 4's only rope source is that single pickup**. A forum post from 2006 describes a rope section with three gaps where the third is "much too large" to jump over ([TASVideos forum 62030](https://tasvideos.org/Forum/Posts/62030)). The post does not say which area it refers to. The project's own note places it in Area 4, and the TAS data shows that the 3 ropes in Area 4 come from that pickup. The only known rope-free route is a hard damage boost, and TASVideos lists damage boosting over pits as a general technique ([TASVideos Game Resources](https://tasvideos.org/GameResources/NES/TeenageMutantNinjaTurtles)). That is not something a charity runner should have to do.

The only public TMNT randomizer, tmnt_rando, treats missiles and ropes as progression keys. Without entrance rando it keeps them entirely out of the item shuffle. With entrance rando it forces at least one missile pickup and one rope pickup into Area 1, because otherwise the player can get stranded ([tmnt_rando randomizer.py](https://github.com/abyssonym/tmnt_rando/blob/master/randomizer.py), [README](https://github.com/abyssonym/tmnt_rando)). That confirms what the packs are for: they give access to progress, not resources in general.

## Established AP worlds make the vanilla source depend on the AP item

AP's developer FAQ has two rules that settle the question. **"All items referenced by logic (however hard or soft) must be `progression`"**, and the logic should describe what is fun rather than what is technically possible ([AP apworld dev FAQ](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ArchipelagoMW/Archipelago/main/docs/apworld_dev_faq.md)). None of the established worlds we looked at leaves a free vanilla copy of a progression item in the level. Instead, two patterns recur. ALttP's `BomblessStart` gives "a max of 0 bombs" until a Bomb Upgrade arrives. The bombs in pots and shops stay, but give nothing as long as the capacity is 0 ([ALttP Options.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ArchipelagoMW/Archipelago/main/worlds/alttp/Options.py)). Kirby's Dream Land 3 blocks the ability to take copy abilities at all until the item has been sent ([KDL3 documentation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ArchipelagoMW/Archipelago/main/worlds/kdl3/docs/en_Kirby's%20Dream%20Land%203.md)). OoT's `bombchus_in_logic` adds two guard rails: **"The first found pack will always have 20 chus"**, and refills remain in shops ([OoT Options.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ArchipelagoMW/Archipelago/main/worlds/oot/Options.py)). So the first receipt always gives a usable amount, and you can never run yourself dry.

The FAQ also points out that AP assumes that something that has once become reachable **"stays reachable forever, no matter what"** ([AP apworld dev FAQ](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ArchipelagoMW/Archipelago/main/docs/apworld_dev_faq.md)). Finite ammunition clashes with that assumption unless something refills it. ALttP itself had to fix logic where checks were counted as reachable even though the player could not carry the bombs needed, for example "12 bombs to kill each mini moldorm" on expert health ([AP PR #2357](https://github.com/ArchipelagoMW/Archipelago/pull/2357)).

Our situation differs from ALttP on one crucial point. **We already have a gate at the entrance: the area select menu does not load Area 3 without both packs** (the project's `lua/tmnt_area_select.lua` and the pass bitmask in `apworld/tmnt/client_logic.py`). Since missiles only exist in Area 3, the player can never reach a missile pickup without Missile Pack, and so the pack is not fake. It does exactly what the logic says: it opens Area 3. Vanilla pickups inside the area become bonus ammo of the same kind as OoT's refill shops, and they protect against firing everything off. This relies on one thing that should be verified in BizHawk: **that the game's natural transition after the Area 2 boss does not load Area 3 past the menu.** If it does, the client needs to catch the transition the same way the menu does.

### The five alternatives compared

| Alternative | What it means for us | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Soft gating | The logic requires the packs but vanilla pickups work freely | **In practice already our model, and it is sound thanks to the menu.** Missiles: yes. Ropes in Area 4: see the next section. |
| (b) Hard gating | The client zeroes the counters every frame, or pickups are removed, until the pack is present | Unnecessary for missiles since the menu already closes off Area 3. Harmful for ropes: Area 4 becomes unbeatable without Rope Pack, which the logic does not know about. |
| (c) Vanilla pickups as AP locations | The MM2 model with `Consumables` as checks ([MM2 options.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ArchipelagoMW/Archipelago/main/worlds/mm2/options.py)) | Removes ammo from the level and increases the risk of running dry. Pickups respawn, so the client has to remember the first pickup itself. Wait. |
| (d) Progressive capacity | Several packs raise the cap, like ALttP's `ShuffleCapacityUpgrades` | The ROM has a fixed cap of 99 and no max variable, so the client would have to emulate the cap. Adds more to read on the tracker with no gain in a short two-player seed. |
| (e) Remove the packs | Area 3 requires only its Sewer Pass | Removes two progression items that can end up in Hyrule. Cross-dependencies between the games are the point of the seed. |

One detail in (c) is worth highlighting. Our `IncludePickups` option itself says that it "only works if the game remembers collected pickups". The game does not remember them, since they respawn. So that option needs client support to work at all. If it is ever enabled, it should start with the pizza and scroll entries as filler checks (around 59 entries) and leave the missile and rope entries untouched.

## The client's gaps are in Area 4 and at continue, not at the Area 3 entrance

**Logic question 1: Area 4.** `AREA_TOOL_REQUIREMENTS` in `apworld/tmnt/rules.py` requires Rope Pack only for Area 3. Area 4 has no rope requirement, even though the notes describe a rope section there and only one vanilla pickup of 3 ropes. With today's client it works in theory: without Rope Pack, 0 is written on load, and the player then picks up 3 ropes in the game. But that only holds if three things are true. The pickup has to come before the gaps. Three ropes have to be enough, that is, 1 rope per gap, which is derived from the code having a single `DEC $A7` and is not tested. And a missed throw has to be fixable by going back to the building. If we ever introduce hard gating (b), Area 4 also becomes unbeatable without Rope Pack while the logic believes the opposite. That is exactly the kind of bug ALttP had to fix in PR #2357. **The recommendation is to add `"Rope Pack"` for Area 4 in `AREA_TOOL_REQUIREMENTS` and in the menu's pass bitmask.** It costs almost nothing, makes Rope Pack a gate for two areas and removes the dependency on an unvalidated pickup placement. At the same time, `start_area: area_4` must preinstall Rope Pack, the same way the test `TestStartArea3` already does for Area 3. The alternative is to play Area 4 in BizHawk without Rope Pack and with deliberately missed throws, and only leave the logic untouched if that goes well.

**Logic question 2: refill after misses and continue.** `full_sync_writes` writes `MISSILE_REFILL=15` and `ROPE_REFILL=5` when an area is loaded, when the console is restarted and when an older savestate is restored. `incremental_writes` refills when the pack is received. In between, nothing happens. The ROM zeroes `$BF`/`$A7` in an init routine that seems to belong to new game or continue (file 0x1C69A). A separate routine zeroes only the missiles (0x1A228), and it is unknown when it runs. If a continue zeroes the counters without the load counter moving, the player is left with 0 missiles in Area 3 despite owning the pack. The vanilla pickups save the situation, and that is a strong reason not to remove them. Still, the most robust solution is a **floor rule in the client: if the pack is owned and the counter is 0, write REFILL.** That makes ammo effectively infinite for whoever owns the pack. On a charity stream that is an advantage, since it meets AP's requirement that what is reachable stays reachable, just like OoT's refill shops. A side note: full sync today *lowers* the counter to 15 if the player has collected more from vanilla pickups. Using `max(current, REFILL)`, as `incremental_writes` already does, would be more consistent.

As an emergency exit during the stream, the host can always run `/send` for an item or `/hint` from the server console ([AP commands guide](https://archipelago.gg/tutorial/Archipelago/commands/en)). A separate YAML option along the lines of `ammo_gating: off/logic_only/enforced` is therefore not needed for this seed.

## Viewers need to see a single line: Hyrule ✔ / New York ✘

Go mode for the TMNT slot is **4/4 turtles and at least 3/5 areas cleared**. That is exactly what is hard to show. "Area N Cleared" are event items without an ID. They are visible in the logic, but no tracker, web tracker or overlay can read them from the server. Anything that is to show area clears has to count the real boss locations (`BOSS_LOCATIONS`) that have been checked, or read a data storage key that the client writes. This applies to PopTracker (`Archipelago:SetNotify`), to a custom overlay and to AP's generic tracker. PopTracker's callback API gives the pack author both item and location IDs ([AUTOTRACKING.md](https://github.com/black-sliver/PopTracker/blob/master/doc/AUTOTRACKING.md)). So the boss locations route works without changes to the world.

**Universal Tracker** is the cheapest route to a logic-aware list for the runner. UT rebuilds the world from YAML or slot_data and lists the locations that are in logic, without the world needing its own tracker code ([AP other tools](https://archipelago.gg/tutorial/Archipelago/other_en), [UT setup.md](https://github.com/FarisTheAncient/Archipelago/blob/tracker/worlds/tracker/docs/setup.md)). Random choices such as the starting area desync UT unless they are sent in slot_data. Our `fill_slot_data` already contains `start_turtle`, `start_area` and `include_pickups`. What is missing is a static `interpret_slot_data`, reading `re_gen_passthrough` in `generate_early`, and `ut_can_gen_without_yaml = True` ([apworld-integration.md](https://github.com/FarisTheAncient/Archipelago/blob/tracker/worlds/tracker/docs/apworld-integration.md), [re-gen-passthrough.md](https://github.com/FarisTheAncient/Archipelago/blob/tracker/worlds/tracker/docs/re-gen-passthrough.md)). Since the area clears are event items, UT works out Shredder's logic automatically. UT follows one slot per window, so ALttP is best handled by the maintained PopTracker pack from StripesOO7. It has AP autotracking and shows per dungeon whether the boss can be beaten ([alttp-ap-poptracker-pack](https://github.com/StripesOO7/alttp-ap-poptracker-pack)). PopTracker loads one pack per window, so two games mean two windows or two OBS sources ([PACKS.md](https://github.com/black-sliver/PopTracker/blob/master/doc/PACKS.md)). A dedicated TMNT pack is estimated at 1–2 days. Its `locations.json` can be reused as a map tab in UT, where "location names must match exactly with AP location names" ([map-integration.md](https://github.com/FarisTheAncient/Archipelago/blob/tracker/worlds/tracker/docs/map-integration.md)).

**In the game**, AP's BizHawk layer already has `display_message()`. It queues `gui.addmessage` in the generic connector ([_bizhawk/__init__.py @0.6.8](https://github.com/ArchipelagoMW/Archipelago/blob/0.6.8/worlds/_bizhawk/__init__.py), [connector_bizhawk_generic.lua](https://github.com/ArchipelagoMW/Archipelago/blob/0.6.8/data/lua/connector_bizhawk_generic.lua)). One call per received item, for example "Got Rope Pack from Hyrule", is half an hour of work. A persistent HUD can be built on `gui.drawText` in our existing Lua and show turtles, passes 1–5, icons for Missile Pack and Rope Pack, areas x/3 and "SHREDDER READY!". According to the project's own tests, `gui.drawText` shows in the window and therefore in OBS's window capture, but not in `client.screenshot`. Put it outside the NES status bar.

**The web and hints.** WebHost gives every game a generic player tracker, a multiworld tracker with a hint table and a sphere tracker. Nice game-specific trackers only exist for hard-coded core games such as ALttP ([tracker.py @0.6.8](https://github.com/ArchipelagoMW/Archipelago/blob/0.6.8/WebHostLib/tracker.py)). So TMNT gets the generic table for free but no logic. It is not verified whether archipelago.gg accepts seeds with a custom apworld, so a self-hosted MultiServer is the safe choice. `start_hints` gives free server hints from the start ([advanced_settings_en.md @0.6.8](https://github.com/ArchipelagoMW/Archipelago/blob/0.6.8/worlds/generic/docs/advanced_settings_en.md)). Putting the three captured turtles there, and perhaps Missile Pack and Rope Pack too, makes the go mode path readable from the first minute. The host's free `/hint` works well as a donation goal: "donate X and we reveal where Leonardo is". With one runner playing both games, a high `progression_balancing` pushes cross-game progression earlier and reduces dead time.

**OBS.** The community's overlays are small hobby projects. The most complete one, Broadcast-Archipelago, has an OBS mode source, CSS per item class, a hint tab and support for several slots at once ([Broadcast-Archipelago](https://github.com/Nic-del/Broadcast-Archipelago)). It will do as a shared item feed in all three scenes if we pin the version and test it. No existing overlay shows both goal conditions. A custom page of about 150 lines can connect as a `Tracker` client to both slots and show "GO MODE: Hyrule ✔ / New York ✘ (turtles 3/4, areas 2/3)". It suits the "Sewers (transition)" scene. The pattern of several games in one overlay has already been tried in [souls-trilogy-tracker](https://github.com/hiKareeem/souls-trilogy-tracker).

## Build order before the stream

The order below ranks by risk to the stream first and then by what viewers get out of it. All effort estimates are our own judgements.

| Prio | What | Why | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rope Pack as a requirement for Area 4 in `rules.py` and the menu's bitmask, plus preinstall on `start_area: area_4` and a test. Alternatively, BizHawk verification that Area 4 can be cleared on the vanilla ropes with one missed throw. | Closes the only known logic hole and makes Rope Pack meaningful in two areas | 1–2 h |
| 2 | Floor rule in the client: pack owned and counter is 0 → REFILL. Full sync with `max(current, REFILL)`. BizHawk test of continue after game over in Area 3. | No softlocks from misses or continue, in line with AP's requirement that reachable stays reachable | 2–3 h |
| 3 | Check that the transition from the Area 2 boss cannot load Area 3 past the menu | The menu is the gate. A back door makes the packs fake. | 1 h |
| 4 | `display_message` on receipt in the TMNT client | Runner and viewers see immediately when a pack arrives from Hyrule | 0.5 h |
| 5 | YAML: `start_hints` for turtles and packs, high `progression_balancing`, spoiler with playthrough for the production team. Self-hosted MultiServer. | Go mode information from the start and an answer key off camera | 0.5 h |
| 6 | UT yamlless support (`interpret_slot_data`, `re_gen_passthrough`, `ut_can_gen_without_yaml`) | Logic-aware checklist for TMNT for the runner | 1–3 h |
| 7 | Lua HUD in BizHawk: turtles, passes, packs, areas x/3, SHREDDER READY | Persistent status in the "New York" scene without an extra window | 0.5 day |
| 8 | Custom go mode panel (browser source, two slots, boss locations as area counter) | The only view that shows both goals. Central to "Sewers (transition)". | 1 day |
| 9 | ALttP PopTracker pack in "Hyrule" and Broadcast-Archipelago as item feed, version-pinned and tested | Ready-made tools that only need setting up | 2–3 h |
| 10 | Dedicated TMNT PopTracker pack, optional, with JSON reused as a UT map tab | Nicer than UT for viewers, but prio 6–8 covers the need | 1–2 days |

Prio 1–8 add up to roughly **2.5–3 days**. Prio 9 adds half a day. Prio 10 we only do if there is time left over.

## Conclusion

The question looked like it was about soft or hard gating, but it has already been settled by a design choice we made for other reasons: the area select menu. When the entrance to the area is the gate, vanilla pickups can stay and act as the game's own safety net. The ammo question then shrinks to two concrete risks: a rope requirement missing in Area 4 and a refill missing after continue. The big uncertainty is empirical, not a matter of design. How many gaps and throws Area 4 requires, and what 0x1A228 and continue do to the counters, can be settled in an evening in BizHawk, not with more research.

The hardest part is what viewers see, because "3 of 5 areas cleared" lives as event items that no tool outside the generator can read. Everything we build for the stream, from the HUD to UT and the OBS panel, therefore has to count boss locations or read a key that the client writes. If that decision is made early, all three display surfaces can share the same source for go mode.
